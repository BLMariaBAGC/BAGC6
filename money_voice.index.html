Your Money Voice

Week 4: Identity and Financial Self-Expression

Introduction
Exercise 1

What does your voice do
when money enters the room?

Not what you think. Not what you believe. What your voice actually does in the moment money is on the table.

Why the voice matters

You can believe in your value entirely and still undermine it the moment you open your mouth. The money voice is where self-worth becomes visible, or disappears. It is the place where weeks of inner work either land or dissolve.

This exercise maps how self-worth shows up in your communication. Not in your intentions, not in your mindset, but in the actual words you say, the words you do not say, and the voice in your head running commentary on all of it.

Your pricing, your negotiating, your ability to ask, to receive, to stand by your number: all of it passes through the money voice first.

This exercise has six steps: identify your money voice archetype, anchor the exercise in a real scenario, reflect on specific money situations, identify your communication pattern, locate where it lives in your body, hear your internal dialogue, understand what fear is underneath it, and finally rewrite your voice from a position of full value.

Opening

Your Money Voice Archetype

Which one recognises itself in you? Choose the archetype that shows up most consistently when money is part of the conversation.

These archetypes are not judgements. They are patterns. Recognising yours immediately is the point of this step. You may see yourself in more than one. Choose the dominant one — the one that leads.

👀
The Minimiser
"Oh it is not that much..."
You downplay your rates, results, or achievements. Making yourself smaller feels safer than being seen fully.
🙏
The Apologiser
"I am sorry, but my fee is..."
You lead with apology. Stating your value feels like an imposition. You pre-empt rejection by apologising for your own worth.
👐
The Avoider
"Let me think about the price..."
Money conversations get deferred, buried in other details, or left until the last possible moment. Avoidance feels like control.
💬
The Overexplainer
"The reason I charge this is..."
You justify at length. Every number needs a rationale. Over-explaining is the nervous system trying to prevent rejection before it arrives.
🎮
The Performer
"Externally confident, internally panicking"
You present confidence convincingly but it is not connected to felt belief. The disconnect between the performance and the inside is exhausting.
⇅
The Deflector
"What do you normally pay for this?"
You redirect money conversations back to others. Asking what they pay, what they expect, or what is standard lets you avoid stating your own number first.
Anchor

Choose Your Scenario

Ground this exercise in a specific real situation. Abstract reflection produces abstract insight. Choose the scenario where your money voice shows up most visibly.

Think of an actual recent conversation, moment, or situation, not a hypothetical. The more specific, the more useful every step that follows will be.

Step 1 of 5

The Reflection

Using the scenario you chose, answer each question as honestly as you can. Not how you think you should feel. How you actually feel.

What happens in your body the moment money enters the conversation?
Before the words come, what does your nervous system do? Any physical sensation, change in breath, shift in posture.
What do you actually say, or not say?
Describe the real behaviour in this scenario. The exact words if you can remember them, or the absence of words.
How do you feel when you receive praise, a compliment, or payment?
Receiving is as revealing as giving. Does value land, or do you immediately diminish it?
Step 2 of 5

Communication Pattern Awareness

Name the pattern your money voice runs. Select all that apply, then describe it in your own words.

The pattern is not the same as the archetype. The archetype is the character. The pattern is the specific behaviour the character produces. Being precise about it is what makes it interruptible.

Step 4 of 5

The Fear Underneath

The money voice is not random. It is protecting you from something. This step names what that is.

These fears are not rational, and they do not need to be. They are old. Most of them were formed long before you had any financial reality to base them on. Naming them does not make them disappear immediately, but it does stop them operating invisibly.

"What am I afraid people will think if I fully express my value?"

Select the fears that resonate, then answer in your own words.

Rejection
Disapproval
Being seen as arrogant
Visibility
Being too much
Losing belonging
Proving the doubters right
Not being able to deliver
People leaving
Success feeling unsafe
Step 5 of 5

The Rewrite

Take one real sentence your money voice uses and rewrite it from the voice of someone who fully believes in their value. This is not an affirmation. It is a practice.

The rewrite should feel slightly uncomfortable. Not unbelievable, but stretching. If it feels completely natural, it has not shifted far enough. If it feels absurd, it has gone too far. Find the version that is true and bigger than what you would normally allow yourself to say.

What you currently say

The actual words your money voice uses. The apologetic, minimising, over-explaining, or silent version.

REWRITTEN AS

What full value expression sounds like

The same information, said by the version of you who fully believes it. Specific. Grounded. Not padded with apology or justification.

Read it out loud

This is not optional. The rewrite needs to leave the page. Say it out loud, alone, more than once. Not to perform it but to hear it. Your nervous system needs to experience the sound of your own full value expression before it can deliver it in the room.

The goal is not to be louder. The goal is to be unedited.
