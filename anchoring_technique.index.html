Building confident access to your peak state

This is not about generating confidence you do not have. It is about training your nervous system to retrieve confidence you have already felt.

What anchoring does

Anchoring is a process from performance psychology used by elite athletes, public speakers, and negotiators to access peak emotional states on demand. It works by creating a direct association between a physical gesture and a specific emotional state, so that activating the gesture retrieves the state automatically.

In this exercise you will identify a memory containing exactly the emotional qualities you want access to in pricing conversations: pride, confidence, certainty. You will build a vivid multi-sensory experience of that memory, amplify the emotion to peak intensity, and then physically store it in a gesture you can use in any high-stakes moment.

The nervous system does not distinguish between a memory and the present moment if the memory is vivid enough. That is the mechanism this exercise uses.

Added in this version: a guided breathing process for amplification, an intensity tracker to confirm you are anchoring at peak state, a state-break between repetitions to deepen each one, a practice tracker for your three to four repetitions, and a future-pacing stage to connect the anchor to the specific conversation you want to use it in. Anchoring without future pacing is like practising a speech without ever placing it in the room. Future pacing closes that gap.

Before you begin: find somewhere quiet where you will not be interrupted for 15 to 20 minutes. This process requires your full attention. Each stage builds on the last. Do not skip ahead.

Stage 1 of 6

Choose Your Memory

The memory does not need to be impressive. It needs to be emotionally intense. A quiet moment of genuine pride is worth more than a public achievement you felt detached from.

You are looking for a moment when you felt genuinely proud, happy, and successful at the same time. All three, together. The memory can be from any age, any context. Learning to ride a bike at seven, finishing something you were not sure you could finish, a moment someone saw you clearly, or a private achievement no one else knew about. What matters is the feeling, not the story.

1
Your Memory
Take a moment before writing. Let the memory come to you rather than searching for it.

Note: if more than one memory comes to mind, choose the one with the strongest physical feeling, not the most impressive story. You are selecting for emotional intensity, not narrative significance.

Stage 2 of 6

Step Into the Memory

Close your eyes and place yourself fully inside it before you write. Work through each sense deliberately. The more vivid the memory, the stronger the anchor.

As you work through each sense, actively intensify what you find. Make the colours brighter. Turn up the sounds. Feel the physical sensations more completely. Let a smile come naturally to your face if one wants to.

Stage 4 of 6

Set the Anchor

You are at peak intensity. Do all four of the following simultaneously, right now, while staying fully inside the feeling.

4
The Physical Anchor
All four steps together, at the peak of the feeling. Do not delay.
Thumb and index finger together, firmly
1
Take a slow, deep breath. Fill your lungs completely. Let the exhale be slow and controlled.
2
Bring your thumb and index finger together firmly on your dominant hand. Press them together with deliberate pressure.
3
Hold the pinch for several seconds while staying completely inside the memory and the feeling. Do not let the image or the emotion fade.
4
When you are ready: open your eyes and release your fingers at the exact same moment. The simultaneous release is important. It marks the end of the anchor-setting.
Stage 5 of 6

Repeat to Strengthen

One repetition creates an association. Three or four repetitions create an automatic response. Each repetition must begin from a clean neutral state.

Between each repetition, do a state break — think of something completely neutral (a piece of furniture, a shopping list, anything mundane) for ten seconds before starting again. This resets your emotional baseline so each new repetition anchors cleanly into the gesture rather than building on residual feeling.

Stage 6 of 6

Future Pacing

Anchoring without future pacing is like rehearsing a performance without ever placing yourself on the actual stage. This stage closes that gap.

Future pacing fires your anchor inside a vivid visualisation of the specific conversation you want to use it in. Your nervous system then stores both: the peak state and the context. The result is that when the real moment arrives, the anchor activates faster and more completely because it has already been there.

▶
Your Pricing Conversation
Bring the specific scenario to mind. Make it as real as the memory.
Visualise the conversation
Close your eyes. Picture the specific person, the specific room or call. See their face, hear the ambient sound, feel where your body is sitting. Make it real. Then open your eyes and describe what you visualised.
Now fire your anchor inside that scene
While holding that visualisation, bring your thumb and index finger together, take the deep breath, and allow the anchored state to rise inside the conversation. Notice what changes. How do you move differently? What do you say? How does the room feel?
What does the anchored version of you say in that moment?
Write the actual words. The anchored version of you speaking your value clearly and without apology.
