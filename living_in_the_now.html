Introduction

Living in the Now

A practice for when your mind will not stop.

What this exercise does

Racing thoughts almost always live in one of two places: the past or the future. We replay what happened, or we rehearse what might. We imagine the worst of our experiences or the worst of what is to come, discounting everything that is still good, still present, still real.

While this is happening, we cannot enjoy the moment we are actually in. We cannot appreciate what is in front of us. And the longer we stay there, the more anxious or low we feel.

Sometimes we feel anxious or depressed because we are living in the past or the future, often imagining the worst, and forgetting all the wonderful things we still have right now.

You cannot drink yesterday's cup of tea. You cannot drink tomorrow's. The only cup you can ever hold is the one in your hands right now.

This exercise will gently bring you back. It begins with a short, clear statement to interrupt the spiral. Then it guides you through your senses, one at a time, so your nervous system has something real to land on. You can have a difficult moment, and still find peace inside it. The present moment is always available.

A Question Worth Asking

Take a moment with each of these before you continue.

Do you think the past is real?
The past has already happened. It no longer exists anywhere in the world. It lives only as a pattern of electrical signals in the synapses of your mind.
Do you think the future is real?
The future has not arrived. It does not yet exist anywhere. It is a projection your mind creates, often shaped more by fear than by fact.

Neither the past nor the future are real right now. They are neurological reactions living in the synapses of your mind.

The only thing that is truly real is this moment.

These three words are yours to use any time your mind starts to spiral. Say them quietly, say them firmly, and then return to what is real: your breath, your body, this room, this second.

Move through each sense, one at a time, noticing only what is real in this moment.

To use this any time

When your thoughts begin to spiral, say the three words: Stop. Stop. Stop. Then bring your attention to one thing you can see, one thing you can hear, and the feeling of your next breath. You do not need to fix the past. You do not need to secure the future. You only need to be here.

A difficult moment is still just a moment. And moments pass. This one is already becoming the next.

You cannot breathe yesterday's breath. You cannot breathe tomorrow's. This breath, right now, is the only one that is real.
