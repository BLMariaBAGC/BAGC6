Exercise 2: Direction

This exercise has four steps: acknowledge the current identity without judgement, define the identity you are becoming, anchor that identity in a concrete behaviour statement, and practise the weekly integration habit that makes it stick.

Step 1 of 4

Acknowledge the Current Identity

Before you can move forward, you honour where you have been. This step removes resistance and shame. It is not a confession. It is a recognition.

The pattern you identified in Exercise 1 was not a flaw. It was a solution. It developed because at some point, it helped you cope. Acknowledging this is what makes genuine change possible, rather than just adding another layer of self-criticism on top of the old one.

I have been someone who...
Be honest and specific. Use your own words, not clinical language.
This made sense because...
What was this pattern protecting? What did it help you avoid or achieve?

Notice: you are not excusing the pattern. You are understanding it. Understanding is what gives you genuine choice over it.

Step 2 of 4

Define Who You Are Becoming

Not who you want to be one day. Who you are in the process of becoming right now. The distinction matters.

Make this grounded and behavioural. Not "I am confident with money" but "I stay calm when my income fluctuates." Not "I am good with finances" but "I make decisions at the right time, not under pressure." The more specific and concrete, the more your nervous system can find it believable.

I have been someone who...

Now
I am becoming someone who...

Examples of grounded identity statements: stays calm when money fluctuates  |  makes decisions at the right time  |  allows income to grow without urgency  |  looks at the numbers clearly rather than avoiding them  |  trusts the plan even when the month feels tight.

Step 3 of 4

Future Behaviour Anchor

Connect your new identity to a specific behaviour. This is the bridge between who you are becoming and what that person actually does.

An identity statement without a behaviour anchor stays abstract. When you describe how this version of you handles money in concrete terms, you give your brain a script to follow. The more specific, the more useful.

This version of me handles money by...
Describe actual behaviours, not feelings. What does this person do, when, and how?

Why this step matters

The old identity had very specific behaviours attached to it. The trigger activated the identity, and the identity ran a predictable script. Your new identity needs its own script, one that you have consciously written rather than unconsciously inherited.

You are not waiting to feel different before you act differently. You are acting as this version of you to accelerate the feeling.
Step 4 of 4

Weekly Integration

One simple practice. Once a week. Three to five lines. No overthinking.

This is not journaling in the traditional sense. There is no requirement for reflection, analysis, or deep insight. It is a single repetition of your new identity statement, written in your own words. Repetition is how identity shifts from intention to instinct.

Your Weekly Identity Statement

Write this once a week. Keep it to three to five lines. Do not overthink it.

Start with "I am the kind of person who..." and let it flow from there
Three to five lines only. This is a repetition, not a reflection
Write from who you are becoming, not who you have been
Do not evaluate or judge what you write. Just write it
