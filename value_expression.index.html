Value Expression and Assertive Communication

Week 4: Identity and Financial Self-Expression

Introduction
Exercise 2 of 2

Confidence is not the problem.

You do not need more confidence before you can express your value. You need to stop letting an old identity decide what you are allowed to say.

Key Insight
"Your difficulty with money conversations is rarely about communication skill. It is usually about identity protection."
"People rarely struggle to communicate value because they lack words. They struggle because part of them still questions whether they are allowed to hold that value."

What this exercise does

Exercise 1 mapped your money voice and the fear underneath it. This exercise moves the dial. Not through forcing confidence you do not feel yet, but through three precise steps that change the identity doing the communicating.

You reframe what expressing value means. You build the identity statement for the version of you who communicates assertively. You choose one concrete behaviour to anchor it in real action this week.

Assertive communication is not aggression. It is the absence of the edits your old identity was making to your true voice.

Three steps: release the permission blockers, build the assertive identity statement, and anchor it in one specific behaviour this week.

Step 1 of 3

Step 2 of 3

The Assertive Identity Statement

Not who you want to be. Who you are in the process of becoming right now, when you communicate from a place of full permission.

Select the communication traits that describe this version of you, then complete the identity statement in your own words. The traits are prompts, not prescriptions. What matters is the sentence you write from them.

Notice the difference between assertive and aggressive. Assertive communication is not louder. It does not push. It holds its ground without apology and without performance. The person on the receiving end of genuine assertive communication often does not notice anything different except that they feel clearer about what they are being offered.

Step 3 of 3

One Action This Week

Identity without action stays abstract. Choose one specific behaviour to practise this week. Not a transformation. A single repetition of the new communication pattern.

The goal of this week's action is not to do it perfectly. The goal is to do it once and notice what happens. One repetition of assertive communication, however imperfect, rewires more than any amount of reflection alone.

State a fee without justifying it
Name your number. Stop. Let the silence exist. Do not add a rationale unless directly asked for one.
Ask for something directly and clearly
A payment, a referral, a decision, support. Ask it in one sentence with no softening language before or after.
Practise silence in a negotiation
After stating your position, stop speaking. Count to five internally before responding to hesitation. Do not rescue the other person from their own pause.
Receive a compliment without deflecting
When someone acknowledges your value, say "thank you" and let it land. No minimising. No redirecting. No "oh it was nothing." Let it be true.
State an increased price to one person this week
Not universally. Just once. To one person. The new number, said calmly, without the old preamble.
Choose your own
If none of these fit your specific situation this week, name the one action that does.
